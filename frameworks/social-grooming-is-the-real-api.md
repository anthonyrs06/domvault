# Social Grooming Is the Real API

> Primates don't scale a troop with a org chart. They scale it with who grooms whom — a living graph of small, costly favors that says who can be trusted when a leopard shows up.

## The connection
In a macaque troop, grooming is not hygiene. It is the protocol: time spent picking nits is expensive, visible, and hard to fake, so the resulting graph is a better trust map than rank. Alliances form along grooming edges; isolation is a missing degree. The same pattern shows up in human systems that look "informal" until they fail — Slack DMs, the person who actually answers a 2am page, the whiskey poured before the hard conversation.

## Why it matters
Most products and orgs instrument the official API (tickets, titles, standups) and miss the grooming graph. That graph is where load actually routes. If you want a new topic, a new team, or a new star to join a universe, the first publish is a grooming move: a small, signed, opt-in gift that others can pull.

## So what
- Map the real edges: who helps whom when there is no ticket.
- Treat a first published note the way a troop treats first grooming — costly enough to mean it, small enough to repeat.
- When a node has no incoming grooms, it is not "early." It is isolated. Go make an edge.
