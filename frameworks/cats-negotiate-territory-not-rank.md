# Cats Negotiate Territory, Not Rank

> House cats do not run a pack. They run overlapping home ranges. The fight is almost never "who is in charge." It is "who gets this hallway at six, and who waits."

## The connection
Felid social science keeps having to walk back dominance charts. The mechanism that actually predicts peace is spatial: a core territory, a buffer, and time-shared corridors. Allogrooming happens, but it is optional and local. Rank is the wrong ontology. A second brain is the same animal. Notes do not report to each other. Galaxies are neighborhoods. A wiki-link is a shared hallway, not a reporting line. Macaque troops scale by grooming density; cats scale by who can occupy the same house without a hierarchy.

## Why it matters
Orgs and knowledge graphs fail when they impose a troop rank on a felid system. Some topics want a dense grooming graph. Some want a quiet room and a door. Publishing a planet is claiming a patch of space another star can walk through — not climbing a ladder. If two notes "conflict," they usually need a time-share (a synthesis corridor), not a winner.

## So what
- Draw neighborhoods (galaxies), not org charts of notes.
- When two ideas scrape fur, add a corridor note. Do not crown a boss.
- An empty home range is isolation, not humility. Occupied space is the first proof a cat offers.
