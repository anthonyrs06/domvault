# Blending Is Honesty About Variance

> Single casks brag; blends admit barrels disagree. A synthesis note that averages two frameworks is a blend, not a cheat.

## The connection
A single barrel can be brilliant or cursed. Distillers know this, so they dump, they blend, they wait until a row of casks can be served as one drink. Bragging about one cask is a sample wearing a medal. A blend is the honest label: barrels disagree, and the pour is what they can agree on. A second brain that ships one hot note as law is selling a souvenir cask. A synthesis note that sits two frameworks in the same glass is a blend. Averaging is not a cheat. It is the warehouse voting.

## Why it matters
Constellations overfit on glowing casks. The public universe should get agreement, not a tasting-room story. Time in the barrel still matters. So does variance. Aging one note forever is still n=1. Linking it until neighbors rhyme, then writing the blend, is how a warehouse tells the truth. Proof is a blend that survived the wood, not a single barrel that survived the marketing.

## So what
- Call a single anecdote a sample. Do not print the label until a second cask agrees.
- Treat a synthesis note as a blend: honest about variance, not a collapse into one winner.
- If two frameworks disagree, pour them together. That is the drink. Bragging about one cask is not.
