# Entry Proof Is Not Exit Proof

> The spirit that went into the barrel is not the spirit that comes out. Capture is entry proof. Publish is exit proof after wood. Pouring the mash is not a vintage.

## The connection
Distillers measure two proofs. Entry proof is what hit the wood. Exit proof is what survived the years, the angel's share, the warehouse weather. They are not the same number and they are not the same drink. A second brain that publishes the capture is pouring entry proof and calling it a vintage. Inbox heat is not barrel heat. The constellation should taste what the wood did, not what the still produced on a Tuesday.

## Why it matters
People treat the first draft as the pour because it already has a title. That is mash in a glass. Time in the rickhouse changes the liquid. Neighbors change the liquid. A planet that never sat is still entry proof wearing a label. The public universe should get exit proof: the claim after climate, not the claim after capture.

## So what
- Do not publish entry proof. Let the wood take a cut before you pour.
- Treat the first draft as a fill number, not as a vintage.
- If the note still tastes like the still, it is not ready. Wait for exit proof.
