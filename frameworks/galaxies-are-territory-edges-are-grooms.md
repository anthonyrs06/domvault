# Galaxies Are Territory, Edges Are Grooms

> Macaques scale by who they sit with. Cats scale by who can share the house without a boss. Two valid graphs. A constellation that picks only one will misread half the animals.

## The connection
[[Cats Negotiate Territory, Not Rank]] says peace is spatial: a core, a buffer, a time-shared hallway. Rank is the wrong ontology. [[Social Grooming Is the Real API]] says load routes through costly, visible favors. Alliances are the grooming graph, not the org chart.

Put them on the same bench and you get a two-layer map. Galaxies are territory: neighborhoods a note can occupy without reporting to a parent. Wiki-links are grooms: optional, local, expensive enough to mean it. Forcing every topic into a troop hierarchy is how you get a cat that never comes out and a macaque with no partners.

A second brain already runs both. Some notes want a quiet sill. Some want a dense troop. The spark was the claim that you need both instruments. The neuron is the claim that they are not competitors. Territory without edges is an empty home range. Edges without territory are a troop with no ground.

## Why it holds
- Spatial peace and social trust are different control loops. Mixing them into "rank" loses both signals.
- Opt-in is load-bearing on both sides. A forced galaxy is a stare. A forced link is a groom you cannot refuse.
- The map should let a cat hold a patch and a macaque hold a partner without crowning a boss.

## So what (the actionable edge)
- Put a note in a galaxy before you demand it groom everyone.
- If two ideas scrape fur, add a corridor note, not a winner.
- Score a cluster by occupied space plus real edges, not by who reports to whom.
