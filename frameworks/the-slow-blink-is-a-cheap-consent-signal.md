# The Slow Blink Is a Cheap Consent Signal

> A cat's hard stare is a threat display. The slow blink is the opposite protocol: I see you, I am not hunting, you may come closer. It is cheap, bidirectional, and easy to refuse.

## The connection
Consent in a constellation is supposed to look like a signed publish. The everyday version is smaller. A spark, a wiki-link, a note held private on purpose — those are slow blinks. Dumping a whole vault into the universe is a stare. Grooming in a macaque troop is costly and public; a cat blink is almost free. You need both. The costly gate (barrel, license, signature) keeps the pour honest. The cheap blink is how two animals agree to close the distance at all.

## Why it matters
Trust systems that only have stares — mandatory reviews, all-to-all sharing, "just make it public" — produce freezing, not approach. Isolates often stare because they never learned the cheap first edge. A White Out works because you can stay home. A blink that cannot be refused is a stare wearing a smile.

## So what
- Offer edges that can be ignored. A spark is a blink. A forced link is a stare.
- Read silence as a refused blink, not as a bug in the other star.
- First contact should be one planet, not a stare into their whole graph.
