# The Buffer Is the Peace

> Core territory plus buffer is how cats share a house. Notes need a buffer — a spark, an unpublished corridor — before they occupy someone else's core. Publishing into a core is a stare.

## The connection
Felids keep a core they will fight for and a buffer they will share. The peace is the buffer, not the friendship. Walk into the core uninvited and you get a stare. A second brain that publishes into another note's core — a merge, a rename, a claim that this is the real version — skips the buffer. Sparks are buffers. Unpublished corridors are buffers. A planet that arrives in someone else's core without a hallway is not a neighbor. It is a trespass wearing a title.

## Why it matters
Knowledge graphs fail when they treat every overlap as a core to capture. Two notes can share a buffer without surrendering a range. The constellation should get planets that claimed a patch and left a hallway, not planets that sat on another star's sill and called it hygiene. A buffer you can refuse is a slow blink. A core you cannot leave is a stare.

## So what
- Keep a buffer between cores. A spark is a hallway. A forced merge is a stare into the nest.
- When two notes scrape fur, widen the buffer. Do not annex the core and call it cleanup.
- Publish into a patch you can hold. Leave the neighboring core alone until there is a treaty.
