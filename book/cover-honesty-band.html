The fastest way to lose an AI deal is to claim the roadmap as the product — and the buyers with real money are exactly the ones whose diligence will catch you. This book is about the other way: keep a visible, engineered gap between what you claim and what you can prove, and police it with machinery. Capability tiers labeled on every output. A calibration gate that locks the predictive feature until realized outcomes clear a threshold set upstream of any deal pressure. Verification briefs that make the pitch pass its own eval. Rubrics, holdouts, go/no-go gauges, an interface that disagrees with your sales instinct — and the closing move: selling the locked feature as the co-development the customer wants to fund.

Honesty isn't the tax on ambition. It's the moat. Drafted by an agent stack — ten agents, one chapter each — from the author's own working notes, in the week the pitch it describes went out.
