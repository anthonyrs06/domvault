Trillions of dollars of assets sit outside every market — not because they're bad, but because nobody can read them. A senior banker put it plainly about medical receivables: nobody trades these pools. This book is about manufacturing the market that doesn't exist yet, with five machines: a standard tape that makes one seller's asset comparable to another's, a reason-coded score strangers can interrogate, provenance a counterparty can verify instead of trust, servicing that travels with the paper, and surveillance that runs at the speed of the data rail — not the monthly trustee report. Then the assembly: originate to distribute, the ghost of 2008 and the holdout discipline that keeps a score honest, Kelly sizing for buyers and builders, and the endgame — basis points on flow for whoever owns the standard.

Markets are not found. They are manufactured. Drafted by an agent stack — ten agents, one chapter each — from the author's own working notes, while the machine it describes was being built.
